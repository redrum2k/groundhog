**What the brief is really testing**

The brief permits an invented company, simulated colleagues, and planted errors. Synthetic data alone is therefore not disqualifying. The challenge is to build a believable company around the agent: competing records, delayed people, limited authority, past decisions that matter later, and evidence connecting decisions to consequences. The submission should distinguish those simulations from genuine execution.

For Groundhog, the strongest thesis is that a custom-data order creates both an operational promise and a financial obligation. The product ties the promise, delivered artifact, objection, remedy, and permission to collect together. The weakest unsupported leap would be turning that bounded demonstration into a claim that an entire finance function, customer relationship, or commercial market has been validated.

Suggested seat sentence: “Groundhog handles order-to-cash coordination and first-line delivery-dispute investigation for Bayline Data, with customer commitments and exceptional financial decisions under explicit human authority.” These are proposed task responsibilities, not evidence that two full-time jobs have been replaced.

Use Groundhog for the agent, Bayline Data for the broker, Summit Fitness for the customer, and Human for the controller. Some historical documents and fixtures use older names and prices. Do not mix those examples into the current commercial story without identifying their context.

**Rehearse these twelve challenges first**

1. **“Which employee would I hire this to be, and what work disappears from their day?”** Give the seat sentence, then name concrete tasks: clarify an order, preserve terms, check delivery, investigate an objection, and prepare an authorized outcome. Do not say “replaces the finance team.” Point to the repeated-owner-question experiment as a narrow measurement, not a headcount calculation.
2. **“Why is this finance rather than a data-quality tool with a payment button?”** Explain that delivery evidence changes whether collection is permitted and whether the provider owes a correction or needs a new agreement. Acknowledge that full accounting and reconciliation across external business systems are outside the demonstrated scope.
3. **“What happens if I give you a scenario you did not prepare?”** Describe the supported domain and evidence, then permit a genuinely unfamiliar in-domain objection. A cited answer, a bounded evidence request, or an honest hold can be appropriate. Do not promise arbitrary autonomous resolution or silently switch to a recording.
4. **“How much of this is real?”** Separate synthetic data and simulated people, actual application execution and model calls, saved execution evidence, mock settlement, and separately reported testnet work. Do not compress them into “all live.”
5. **“Who watches the employee that decides whether its employer deserves payment?”** Explain the separation among factual interpretation, mandatory artifact checks, customer authority, controller decisions and the trusted payment operator. Admit the remaining semantic risk: a wrong finding can influence dispute closure even when the model cannot directly send money.
6. **“If the customer never accepts, do you still collect?”** The aggregate policy permits collection after explicit acceptance OR eligible undisputed review expiry, subject to all remaining gates. The current short review clock is a demo assumption. The slogan “only paid once the customer accepts” is too absolute for that policy.
7. **“Does sending difficult cases to Human simply move the work?”** Say yes, unnecessary escalation is a real product cost. The repo preserves an example of over-escalation. A business pilot must count total review effort, decision quality and delay, not merely unsafe actions prevented.
8. **“What did it actually learn?”** A bounded, reviewed source interpretation procedure, such as which field counts adults and which authorized partitions may be combined. Reuse is constrained by source, schema and authority; explicit approval and activation precede it. This does not establish that the source owner told the truth.
9. **“Your counts match the source. How do you know the source is right?”** Distinguish reproducibility, contractual conformity and real-world validity. Aggregate data may be unable to establish identity-level deduplication. Do not claim that a citation or checksum resolves that evidence gap.
10. **“Who has agreed to buy this, and what will they pay?”** The reviewed evidence does not establish customers, willingness to pay or ROI. The $1 order price and expansion prices are demo assumptions. Offer a proposed design-partner test, not an invented sales result.
11. **“Show a failure and explain why you would still hire it.”** Use a preserved false escalation or a repaired interpretation/evidence-routing failure. Explain the financial containment, remaining customer cost, and the exact narrower qualification that followed. A safe failure remains a quality failure.
12. **“What is the moment?”** Choose one evidence-backed business decision: refusing to invent missing identity evidence and holding collection; contradicting a false count allegation with the delivered row; or reusing approved source knowledge on a fresh order. For a defect-and-repair story, disclose that the recorded missing-ZIP test injects a fault caught before publication. Do not tell it as a real customer's live-model dispute.

**The full question bank**

The follow-ups below deliberately press beyond the first polished answer. They test business judgment rather than code or infrastructure choices.

6. Is Groundhog an employee, a copilot or an automated workflow? Follow-up: what work does it pick up without another prompt, and where does it stop?
7. Why does order-to-cash include producing the dataset in your demonstration? Follow-up: could the financial role work with an existing delivery team?
8. Who is Groundhog's manager, and what would that manager review weekly? Follow-up: what would count as poor performance even if no money was lost?
9. What would you explicitly refuse to include in this employee's job description? Follow-up: can it negotiate prices, make concessions or promise a delivery date?

What a strong answer needs: a narrow job description, a real workflow, named decision owners, and measurable responsibilities. The brief asks for a few related seats; it does not require pretending that every finance task is implemented.

**Fourteen reasoning traps judges can expose**

| Tempting pitch claim | Why it is vulnerable | More defensible wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “It replaces three employees.” | Counting capabilities does not establish full job coverage or eliminated workload. | “It handles these specified tasks; staffing impact needs a pilot.” |
| “It is fully autonomous.” | Buyers, source experts and controllers supply essential authority and information. | “It advances eligible work autonomously within defined permissions.” |
| “It only gets paid after acceptance.” | The aggregate policy also permits eligible undisputed expiry. The money is Bayline's, not the agent's. | “It collects for Bayline only when the agreed evidence and review conditions permit.” |
| “All checks passed, so the data is true.” | Agreement with synthetic sources does not validate real-world measurement or hidden assumptions. | “The delivered artifact meets the checked terms against the accepted sources.” |
| “Independent verification proves it cannot be wrong.” | Separate software can share incorrect assumptions; same-model review is not independent human judgment. | “Independent byte checks and bounded review constrain error; semantic risk remains.” |
| “The model cannot move money, so its mistakes cannot cost money.” | A mistaken finding can still affect dispute closure and downstream eligibility. | “The model has no direct payment authority; semantic decisions still require risk evaluation.” |
| “Escalation means the system succeeded.” | A hold may be financially safe while unnecessarily wasting time and delaying collection. | “It contained the risk; we separately measure whether escalation was necessary.” |
| “It learned, so it saves staff time.” | Avoided questions do not include governance and maintenance costs. | “Repeated simulated owner questions fell from two to zero in the measured later orders.” |
| “It remembers, therefore it improves itself.” | Durable scoped procedure reuse is not autonomous policy improvement or model retraining. | “It reuses qualified, explicitly approved source knowledge while its applicability holds.” |
| “The workflow is end-to-end, so finance is covered.” | No demonstrated complete accounting, tax, external ledger, close or refund operation. | “This bounded order-to-cash slice connects fulfillment evidence to permitted collection.” |
| “Synthetic data makes the demo invalid.” | The brief explicitly permits constructed worlds and planted errors. | “The question is whether the world is realistic, disclosed and evaluated without answer leakage.” |
| “Blockchain makes it trustless.” | The operator applies application rules; source quality and commercial authority remain off-chain judgments. | “Escrow enforces specified payment mechanics within an explicitly trusted operating model.” |
| “Three out of three means 100% reliable.” | Tiny author-defined cohorts and repeated development cannot estimate general reliability. | “These three frozen engineering cases passed; earlier misses remain disclosed.” |
| “The customer was wrong, so the job is done.” | Contractual correctness may leave a legitimate usability or relationship problem. | “The evidence resolves the allegation; account management may still require a separate commercial response.” |

**Twelve judge-supplied scenarios to rehearse**

These are proposed rehearsal probes, not claims that this review executed them. Outcomes must follow the actual accepted terms and evidence; do not preload a verdict merely to match this table.

| Judge's intervention | The business distinction to defend | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “ZIP 94111 has zero adults.” | A false allegation should be addressed from the actual delivered row; dismissing it alone does not authorize payment. | Accepted order, row value, source citation, finding, separate payment conditions. |
| “Prove nobody is counted in two ZIPs.” | A contractual disjointness assumption is not identity-level empirical evidence. | Missing-evidence explanation, bounded questions, unresolved hold and any separately authorized remedy. |
| “Does yoga count?” | Asking about scope is not buying more scope. | Cited answer tied to this agreement; no new charge or silent amendment. |
| “Yoga was included, but you left it out.” | Already-owed work is not an upsell. | Accepted definition, source evidence, defect determination and permitted remedy. |
| “Add Oakland too.” | New scope needs a separate offer, explicit acceptance and funding. | Parent unchanged; child work starts only when eligible. |
| “The blank cell proves there are zero people.” | Suppressed, unknown and zero are different. | Status, public-safe count and accepted suppression terms; no exposure of hidden exact counts. |
| “The count is wrong, the people overlap, and I also want Oakland.” | Compound allegations and a new purchase request must not collapse into one verdict. | Each claim retained; unresolved elements block inappropriate closure. This is a known area of semantic risk. |
| “The source owner has withdrawn the explanation you learned.” | New work must respect current authority; history must not be silently rewritten. | Hold on unsafe reuse, version applicability and preserved accepted records. |
| Human says, “Return the money as goodwill; we still don't know who is right.” | Commercial settlement does not establish factual truth. | Bounded return authority and an unchanged UNKNOWN finding. |
| “Human has left for the weekend.” | No response is not approval; expiring authority is not returned money. | Pending case, remaining permitted actions and honest customer-facing status. |
| Refresh or interrupt after the payment request, then repeat it. | A repeated attempt must not become another obligation or collection. | Same order and operation, accurate pending/confirmed state, one financial effect. |
| “I found a defect after you already collected.” | A recorded complaint is not an implemented refund. | Preserved complaint, human handoff and candid disclosure that post-capture refund execution is unimplemented. |

The best opening moment for this track is a decision that changes what the company is allowed to do. A good candidate is the missing identity-evidence case: the agent declines to invent proof, collection stays held, and Human can make a recorded commercial decision without rewriting the facts. The false-count case gives the contrasting outcome: it can defend Bayline when the evidence supports that. Reuse of approved knowledge then addresses the brief's longer-term company-memory theme.

Choose a case that is available and inspectable in the actual demo mode. A saved genuine execution is useful proof, but it must be labeled saved execution; it does not replace the brief's requested live challenge. The retained rehearsal's defect narrative and the primary aggregate verifier's before-publication correction are different stories.

**How to rehearse the answers**

Answer in four moves: the business decision, the boundary on authority, a specific piece of evidence, and the remaining limit. For example: “It can investigate whether the delivered count contradicts the complaint. It cannot treat that finding as buyer consent or unconditional payment permission. Here are the accepted terms, row and payment record from this order. That demonstrates this case; it does not establish general dispute accuracy.”

For each question, score the response informally as 0 if it is a slogan, 1 if it explains a plausible policy, 2 if it links the policy to a specific implementation or observed case, and 3 if it also names the limitation and what would change the decision. This is a rehearsal device, not Hyphenate's scoring system.

Practice the twelve priority challenges first, then the compound-claim, unavailable-controller, source-revocation and post-collection-defect scenarios. Have someone outside the presenting pair choose the wording. Keep a concise disclosure available: “The sources and company roles are simulated. These are actual application records, with real model calls where indicated. This combined qualification uses mock payments; separate payment experiments are identified separately.” Adapt that sentence to the mode actually running.
